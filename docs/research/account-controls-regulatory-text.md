# What the current authoritative regulatory texts say about user accounts, authority checks, session handling and account deactivation in a computerised laboratory system

**Asked by:** Orchestrator — account/role model question for 20+ named laboratory users
**Date:** 2026-09-11
**Status of sources:** This is agent-gathered primary-source text, retrieved and quoted verbatim on 2026-09-11. It is **not** a compliance determination, gap analysis, or recommendation. Validation of the system referenced by this ticket is explicitly deferred; this document exists only so that the account model does not foreclose requirements that will eventually apply. Every source below was fetched directly (eCFR API, FDA PDF, PIC/S PDF, European Commission PDF) on the retrieval date, not taken from a secondary write-up. Currency was checked against known traps: the FDA guidance quoted is the **final December 2018** version (confirmed from the document's own text, not a superseded draft); EU GMP Annex 11 quoted is **Revision 1, in force since 30 June 2011**, confirmed still current as of the retrieval date (a revision was out for public consultation July–October 2025 with adoption not expected before 2026/2027 — see "What I could not establish"); NIST HB 150 is not cited anywhere in this document and was not consulted.

## Findings

### 1. 21 CFR Part 11 (electronic records; electronic signatures)

Source: eCFR, Title 21, Part 11, version as of 2026-09-09 (the most recent issue date available), retrieved via `https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-09-09/title-21.xml?part=11`, canonical browsing URL `https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-11`.

A threshold fact governs how to read all of Part 11: **§11.10 ("Controls for closed systems") applies to any closed electronic-record system**, not only to systems that carry electronic signatures. Its opening clause states: "Persons who use closed systems to create, modify, maintain, or transmit electronic records shall employ procedures and controls designed to ensure the authenticity, integrity, and, when appropriate, the confidentiality of electronic records, and to ensure that the signer cannot readily repudiate the signed record as not genuine." The "signer" language in the trailing clause presupposes signatures may exist, but the enumerated controls (a)–(k) are stated as obligations on any closed system used for electronic records — signatures are not a precondition for §11.10 to bind. By contrast, **Subpart C (§§11.100, 11.200, 11.300) binds only when electronic signatures are in use** — each of those sections opens by referring to "electronic signature(s)" specifically.

- **§11.10(d)** — "Limiting system access to authorized individuals." Binds any closed system (Subpart B), independent of whether signatures are used.
- **§11.10(g)** — "Use of authority checks to ensure that only authorized individuals can use the system, electronically sign a record, access the operation or computer system input or output device, alter a record, or perform the operation at hand." Binds any closed system; the electronic-signature clause is one of several alternatives it lists, not a precondition for the whole subsection.
- **§11.10(h)** — "Use of device (e.g., terminal) checks to determine, as appropriate, the validity of the source of data input or operational instruction." Binds any closed system, "as appropriate" — the regulation itself makes this conditional on appropriateness, not on signature use.
- **§11.10(i)** — "Determination that persons who develop, maintain, or use electronic record/electronic signature systems have the education, training, and experience to perform their assigned tasks." Text explicitly covers "electronic record/electronic signature systems" — binds regardless of whether the specific record in question is signed.
- **§11.100(a)** — "Each electronic signature shall be unique to one individual and shall not be reused by, or reassigned to, anyone else." This is in Subpart C ("Electronic Signatures") and by its own terms binds only where electronic signatures are used.
- **§11.200(a)** — governs "Electronic signature components and controls," binding only where signatures not based on biometrics are used. The relevant text: "(1) Employ at least two distinct identification components such as an identification code and password. (i) When an individual executes a series of signings during a single, continuous period of controlled system access, the first signing shall be executed using all electronic signature components; subsequent signings shall be executed using at least one electronic signature component that is only executable by, and designed to be used only by, the individual. (ii) When an individual executes one or more signings not performed during a single, continuous period of controlled system access, each signing shall be executed using all of the electronic signature components." This is the regulation's own device for distinguishing a continuous logged-in session (lighter re-authentication on repeat signings) from a session that has been broken (full re-authentication required) — but it is a signing control, not a general session-timeout rule, and it applies only when electronic signatures are used.
- **§11.300(a)–(e)** — "Persons who use electronic signatures based upon use of identification codes in combination with passwords shall employ controls to ensure their security and integrity." Binds only where ID-code/password-based electronic signatures are used. Quoted in full:
  - (a) "Maintaining the uniqueness of each combined identification code and password, such that no two individuals have the same combination of identification code and password."
  - (b) "Ensuring that identification code and password issuances are periodically checked, recalled, or revised (e.g., to cover such events as password aging)."
  - (c) "Following loss management procedures to electronically deauthorize lost, stolen, missing, or otherwise potentially compromised tokens, cards, and other devices that bear or generate identification code or password information, and to issue temporary or permanent replacements using suitable, rigorous controls."
  - (d) "Use of transaction safeguards to prevent unauthorized use of passwords and/or identification codes, and to detect and report in an immediate and urgent manner any attempts at their unauthorized use to the system security unit, and, as appropriate, to organizational management."
  - (e) "Initial and periodic testing of devices, such as tokens or cards, that bear or generate identification code or password information to ensure that they function properly and have not been altered in an unauthorized manner."

**No provision in Part 11 mentions an inactivity timeout, automatic logoff, or forced re-authentication after idle time.** §11.200(a)(1)(i)-(ii) addresses re-signing within or across a "continuous period of controlled system access" but never defines that period by a clock, and never requires the session to end after inactivity.

### 2. 21 CFR Part 211 (current good manufacturing practice for finished pharmaceuticals)

Source: eCFR, Title 21, Part 211, version 2026-09-09, `https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-09-09/title-21.xml?part=211`.

- **§211.194(a)(7)** — "The initials or signature of the person who performs each test and the date(s) the tests were performed."
- **§211.194(a)(8)** — "The initials or signature of a second person showing that the original records have been reviewed for accuracy, completeness, and compliance with established standards."
  These two clauses are the textual basis for "who performed the test" and "who reviewed it" attribution; they say nothing about accounts, logins, or system access — they require the record to identify the individual, by whatever medium the record is kept in.
- **§211.22** — establishes the quality control unit's authority to approve/reject and to review production records "to assure that no errors have occurred," and requires (d) that QC unit responsibilities and procedures "be in writing; such written procedures shall be followed." No account/role language.
- **§211.25** — "Personnel qualifications." (a) requires that "each person engaged in the manufacture, processing, packing, or holding of a drug product shall have education, training, and experience... to enable that person to perform the assigned functions," with CGMP training "conducted by qualified individuals on a continuing basis." (b) extends the same standard to supervisory personnel. (c) requires "an adequate number of qualified personnel." This section is about training records and qualification, not system accounts — but is the regulatory hook FDA's 2018 guidance cites (via §11.10(i)-style reasoning) when discussing who may be authorized to access a system.

### 3. FDA "Data Integrity and Compliance With Drug CGMP — Questions and Answers," Guidance for Industry

Source: `https://www.fda.gov/media/119267/download`, confirmed final, dated **December 2018** on its own cover/footer text (not the November 2018 draft, and not the earlier April 2016 draft). Retrieved and text-extracted 2026-09-11.

- **Question 4, "How should access to CGMP computer systems be restricted?"** — "You must exercise appropriate controls to assure that changes to computerized MPCRs or other CGMP records or input of laboratory data into computerized records can be made only by authorized personnel (§ 211.68(b))... FDA recommends that you restrict the ability to alter specifications, process parameters, data, or manufacturing or testing methods by technical means where possible (e.g., by limiting permissions to change settings or data). The system administrator role, including any rights to alter files and settings, should be assigned to personnel independent from those responsible for the record content. To assist in controlling access, it is important that manufacturers establish and implement a method for documenting authorized personnel's access privileges for each CGMP computer system in use (e.g., by maintaining a list of authorized individuals) (see § 211.68(b))."
- **Question 5, "Why is FDA concerned with the use of shared login accounts for computer systems?"** — "When login credentials are shared, a unique individual cannot be identified through the login and the system would not conform to the CGMP requirements in parts 211 and 212. FDA requires that system controls, including documentation controls, be designed in accordance with CGMP to assure product quality (e.g., §§ 211.100 and 212.50). For example, you must implement documentation controls that ensure that the actions as described in question 4 are attributable to a specific individual (see §§ 211.68(b), 211.188(b)(11), 211.194(a)(7) and (8), and 212.50(c)(10)). Shared, read-only user accounts that do not allow the user to modify data or settings are acceptable for viewing data, but they do not conform with the part 211 and 212 requirements for actions, such as second person review, to be attributable to a specific individual."

This is the guidance's clearest statement on the account model: a shared write-capable account is treated as non-conforming, a shared **read-only** account is explicitly carved out as acceptable, and administrator rights are expected to sit with personnel independent of the people generating/reviewing the record content. **No Q&A in this document mentions session inactivity timeouts, automatic logoff, or workstation re-authentication.** A targeted search of the extracted text for "session," "timeout," "inactiv," and "log off/logoff" in this document returned no matches outside the shared-login Q&A above.

### 4. EU GMP Annex 11 — Computerised Systems

Source: European Commission, EudraLex Volume 4, `https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/annex11_01-2011_en_0.pdf`. Document header identifies it as "Status of the document: revision 1," "Deadline for coming into operation: 30 June 2011." This is the version currently in force (see currency note below).

- **§2, Personnel** (full text): "There should be close cooperation between all relevant personnel such as Process Owner, System Owner, Qualified Persons and IT. All personnel should have appropriate qualifications, level of access and defined responsibilities to carry out their assigned duties."
- **§9, Audit Trails** (full text): "Consideration should be given, based on a risk assessment, to building into the system the creation of a record of all GMP-relevant changes and deletions (a system generated 'audit trail'). For change or deletion of GMP-relevant data the reason should be documented. Audit trails need to be available and convertible to a generally intelligible form and regularly reviewed." (Note: this text conditions audit trail creation on a risk assessment — it does not make audit trails an unconditional requirement, in this currently-in-force version.)
- **§12, Security** (full text, verbatim):
  - "12.1 Physical and/or logical controls should be in place to restrict access to computerised system to authorised persons. Suitable methods of preventing unauthorised entry to the system may include the use of keys, pass cards, personal codes with passwords, biometrics, restricted access to computer equipment and data storage areas."
  - "12.2 The extent of security controls depends on the criticality of the computerised system."
  - "12.3 Creation, change, and cancellation of access authorisations should be recorded."
  - "12.4 Management systems for data and for documents should be designed to record the identity of operators entering, changing, confirming or deleting data including date and time."

§12.3 is the specific clause bearing on account lifecycle: it requires that the *creation, change, and cancellation* of access authorizations be recorded — i.e., there should be a record when an account is granted, modified, or revoked, though the text does not specify a triggering event (such as an employee's departure) or a time limit for revocation. §12.4 requires operator identity to be recorded against actions on data, not merely against sign-off. **No clause in this version of Annex 11 mentions session timeout or automatic logoff.**

### 5. PIC/S PI 041-1 — "Good Practices for Data Management and Integrity in Regulated GMP/GDP Environments"

Source: `https://picscheme.org/docview/4234`, confirmed final text, cover page reads "PI 041-1, 1 July 2021," 63 pages, PDF version 1.5. (Not the November 2018 Draft 3, which is a different, superseded document also found in search results.)

Section 9.5, "System security for computerised systems," is the operative section. Quoted:

- On shared/generic accounts: "Individual Login IDs and passwords should be set up and assigned for all staff needing to access and utilise the specific electronic system. Shared login credentials do not allow for traceability to the individual who performed the activity. For this reason, shared passwords, even for reasons of financial savings, should be prohibited."
- On unique identity in user lists: "Systems should be able to generate a list of users with actual access to the system, including user identification and roles. User lists should include the names or unique identifiers that permit identification of specific individuals. The list should be used during periodic user reviews."
- On privilege segregation: "Granting of administrator access rights to computerised systems and infrastructure used to run GMP/GDP critical applications should be strictly controlled. Administrator access rights should not be given to normal users on the system (i.e. segregation of duties)... User access controls should ensure strict segregation of duties (i.e. that all users on a system who are conducting normal work tasks should have only normal access rights). Normally, users with elevated access rights (e.g. admin) should not conduct normal work tasks on the system." And: "System administrators should normally be independent from users performing the task, and have no involvement or interest in the outcome of the data generated or available in the electronic system. For example, QC supervisors and managers should not be assigned as the system administrators for electronic systems in their laboratories (e.g. HPLC, GC, UV-Vis)." A carve-out is given for smaller organisations: "it may be permissible for a nominated person in the quality unit or production department to hold access as the system administrator; however, in these cases the administrator access should not be used for performing routine operations and the user should hold a second and restricted access for performing routine operations. In these cases all administrator activities conducted should be recorded and approved within the quality system."
- On session inactivity (see sub-question 6 below for full analysis): "Computerised systems giving access to GMP/GDP critical data or operations should have an inactivity logout, which, either at the application or the operating system level, logs out a user who has been inactive longer than a predefined time. The time should be shorter, rather than longer and should typically be set to prevent unauthorised access to systems. Upon activation of the inactivity logout, the system should require the user to go through the normal authentication procedure to login again."
- On legacy single-login systems: "It is acknowledged that some legacy computerised systems support only a single user login or limited numbers of user logins. Where no suitable alternative computerised system is available, equivalent control may be provided by third party software, or a paper based method of providing traceability (with version control). The suitability of alternative systems should be justified and documented. Increased data review is likely to be required for hybrid systems."
- On password change control: "Systems where a new password cannot be changed by the user, but can only be created by the admin, are incompatible with data integrity, as the confidentiality of passwords cannot be maintained."

**On records attributable to a user who has left the organisation:** I searched the full extracted text of PI 041-1 for language on departed/terminated staff, deactivation triggered by departure, and could not find any clause addressing this specifically. The nearest related text is generic (network-level) access-review language in the network-security section — "Processes should be in place for the authorisation, monitoring and removal of access" — which is stated in the context of firewall/network access, not user-account lifecycle tied to employment status. PI 041-1 does not appear to specify what happens, procedurally or to the record, when an account's owner leaves.

### 6. The inactivity-timeout / automatic-logoff sub-question — resolved

This is worth stating precisely because industry practice repeats an "automatic logoff requirement" as if it is uniform across the primary texts, and it is not.

- **21 CFR Part 11 does not mention inactivity timeout, automatic logoff, or re-authentication after idle time anywhere in its text** (checked the full text of Subparts A, B, and C above).
- **21 CFR Part 211 does not mention it.**
- **FDA's December 2018 Data Integrity Q&A does not mention it** (checked; the shared-login Q&A is the closest related topic and does not raise timeouts).
- **EU GMP Annex 11 (Revision 1, 2011, currently in force) does not mention it.**
- **PIC/S PI 041-1 (1 July 2021) does explicitly require it**, in Section 9.5 quoted above: "Computerised systems giving access to GMP/GDP critical data or operations should have an inactivity logout... The time should be shorter, rather than longer... Upon activation of the inactivity logout, the system should require the user to go through the normal authentication procedure to login again."

So: the commonly cited "automatic logoff requirement" is real, but its textual home among the sources checked here is specifically **PIC/S PI 041-1, Section 9.5**, an inspectorate-good-practices guidance document, not 21 CFR Part 11, not 21 CFR Part 211, not FDA's 2018 Q&A guidance, and not the currently-in-force EU GMP Annex 11. Where a practitioner cites this as a "GMP requirement" without naming PIC/S PI 041-1, the citation is imprecise, since three of the five primary sources checked are silent on it.

## What I could not establish

- **EU GMP Annex 11 revision status**: a draft revision was released for public consultation 7 July 2025–7 October 2025 and industry commentary projects EMA/EC/PIC/S adoption steps through 2026, with some sources projecting the final text is not in force until Q1 2027. I could not reach an adopted final replacement text as of the retrieval date; the quoted §12.1–12.4 above is the version I confirmed is still the one published as current EudraLex Volume 4 content. If the orchestrator needs certainty about whether a newer Annex 11 has since been adopted, that needs to be re-checked closer to the decision date, not assumed frozen from this document.
- **PIC/S PI 041-1 and departed-user accounts**: as noted, I could not find text in PI 041-1 addressing what should happen to accounts, or to records attributable to accounts, of individuals who have left the organisation. This may be addressed elsewhere in PIC/S guidance (e.g., PI 011, "Good Practices for Computerised Systems in Regulated GxP Environments") which I did not fetch for this question, or it may simply not be addressed by name in the current data-integrity guidance.
- **§11.200(a)'s "continuous period of controlled system access"** is not defined by a clock or session-length figure anywhere in Part 11's text; I could not find any FDA interpretive text (e.g., in the 2018 guidance) that quantifies it, and did not search beyond the two documents named in the task for further FDA interpretation of that phrase.
- I did not fetch ICH Q7, 21 CFR Part 212 (PET drugs, referenced in passing by the FDA guidance and by §11.1(k)), or PIC/S PI 011, none of which were named in the task; if those turn out to be load-bearing for the account decision, they are not covered here.
