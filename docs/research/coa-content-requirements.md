# Which authoritative primary sources impose content requirements on a Certificate of Analysis (or equivalent issued laboratory report), and what exactly do they require?

**Asked by:** Orchestrator, follow-on to the prior scouting pass establishing that 21 CFR Part 211 imposes no CoA content requirements
**Date:** 2026-09-11
**Status of sources:** ICH Q7 text was read from FDA's own hosted PDF of the current guidance (September 2016, Revision 1, incorporating ICH Q7/Q7A with the FDA-adopted Q&A). WHO TRS 1010 Annex 4 (2018) was read as the current, most recent WHO model CoA — I checked the annex's own text and found it supersedes the 2002 TRS 902 Annex 10 model, which is listed there only as a superseded prior version ("first published in 2002 ... the current model is shown in Appendix 1"), so TRS 1010 Annex 4 is the current WHO text, not TRS 902. EU GMP Volume 4 Part I Chapter 6 (Quality Control) was read from the European Commission's own hosted PDF, dated 28 March 2014, into operation 1 October 2014; I could not independently confirm from the Commission's website whether a newer revision of Chapter 6 exists — see "What I could not establish." EU GMP Annex 16 was read from the European Commission's own hosted final PDF (dated October 2015, into operation 15 April 2016). 21 CFR 11.10(b) was read from eCFR's own current rendering, fetched today. USP General Notices was confirmed paywalled on attempted retrieval — see finding 4 below; no USP text is reproduced or paraphrased anywhere in this document.

## Findings

### 1. ICH Q7, Section 11.4 "Certificates of Analysis" — the load-bearing source

Source: *Q7 Good Manufacturing Practice Guidance for Active Pharmaceutical Ingredients — Guidance for Industry*, U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, FDA (CDER/CBER), September 2016, ICH Revision 1. Retrieved directly from FDA: https://www.fda.gov/files/drugs/published/Q7-Good-Manufacturing-Practice-Guidance-for-Active-Pharmaceutical-Ingredients-Guidance-for-Industry.pdf (primary text read in full, not a secondary summary).

Section 11.4, clauses 11.40–11.44, verbatim:

- **11.40**: "Authentic certificates of analysis should be issued for each batch of intermediate or API on request."
- **11.41**: "Information on the name of the intermediate or API including, where appropriate, its grade, the batch number, and the date of release should be provided on the certificate of analysis. For intermediates or APIs with an expiry date, the expiry date should be provided on the label and certificate of analysis. For intermediates or APIs with a retest date, the retest date should be indicated on the label and/or certificate of analysis."
- **11.42**: "The certificate should list each test performed in accordance with compendial or customer requirements, including the acceptance limits, and the numerical results obtained (if test results are numerical)."
- **11.43**: "Certificates should be dated and signed by authorized personnel of the quality unit(s) and should show the name, address, and telephone number of the original manufacturer. Where the analysis has been carried out by a repacker or reprocessor, the certificate of analysis should show the name, address, and telephone number of the repacker/reprocessor and reference the name of the original manufacturer."
- **11.44**: "If new certificates are issued by or on behalf of repackers/reprocessors, agents or brokers, these certificates should show the name, address and telephone number of the laboratory that performed the analysis. They should also contain a reference to the name and address of the original manufacturer and to the original batch certificate, a copy of which should be attached."

Related clauses elsewhere in the same guidance, both cross-referencing 11.4:
- Section 17 ("APIs and Intermediates Distributed by Agents, Brokers, Traders, Distributors, Repackers, or Relabelers"), clause **17.63**: "The specific guidance for certificate of analysis included in Section 11.4 should be met."
- Section 17.2, a records-retention list, includes "All authentic Certificates of Analysis, including those of the original manufacturer" among records an agent/broker must keep.

**Scope note:** ICH Q7 governs Active Pharmaceutical Ingredients (APIs) and intermediates, not finished dosage-form drug products. FDA has adopted it as guidance; it is non-binding ("Contains Nonbinding Recommendations," printed on every page of the PDF) rather than a codified regulation. It is the only source found in this scouting pass that specifies CoA content in the kind of clause-by-clause detail the question asked about.

**What Q7 does *not* say:** Section 11.4 does not use the word "reissue," and does not describe a procedure, numbering scheme, or required reason for correcting or superseding a certificate found to be wrong after issuance — see the distinct finding below.

### 2. EU GMP — Part I Chapter 6 (Quality Control) and Annex 16

Source for Chapter 6: EudraLex Volume 4, *EU Guidelines for Good Manufacturing Practice for Medicinal Products for Human and Veterinary Use*, Part 1, Chapter 6: Quality Control, European Commission, Brussels, 28 March 2014, into operation 1 October 2014. Retrieved from the Commission's own document repository: https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/c74c8720-27bf-4252-808f-d65a206a90bb_en?filename=2014-11_vol4_chapter_6.pdf (primary text read in full).

Chapter 6 does **not** set out CoA content requirements comparable to ICH Q7 §11.4. It mentions certificates of analysis only twice, both in passing, as items to be kept on file or referenced rather than as a spec for what a CoA must contain:
- §6.7(v), in a list of documentation the Quality Control department must have readily available: "Testing reports and/or certificates of analysis."
- §6.17(iv), in a list of what a laboratory test record must include: "Test results, including observations and calculations, and reference to any certificates of analysis."

Chapter 6's own content list is for the underlying **test record** (§6.17, items i–ix: material/product name and dosage form; batch number and manufacturer/supplier; references to specifications and test procedures; test results with observations/calculations and reference to any CoA; dates of testing; initials of the tester; initials of the verifier; a clear statement of approval/rejection with dated signature of the responsible person; reference to equipment used) — this is a record-content requirement, not a CoA-content requirement, and it is the internal laboratory record, not the document issued outward.

I could not confirm from the Commission's website whether a Chapter 6 revision postdating 28 March 2014 exists; the Commission's own EudraLex Volume 4 index page, fetched today, lists this 2014-11 PDF without stating explicitly that it is the current version (see "What I could not establish").

Source for Annex 16: EudraLex Volume 4, Annex 16, *Certification by a Qualified Person and Batch Release*, European Commission, version dated October 2015 ("v4_an16_201510_en"), into operation 15 April 2016. Retrieved from the Commission's own repository: https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/v4_an16_201510_en_0.pdf (primary text read in full).

Annex 16 governs **QP certification and batch release**, which this project treats as out of scope; it is reported here only insofar as it bears on CoA content or reliance. Annex 16's document is a **"Batch Certificate,"** a distinct instrument from a laboratory Certificate of Analysis:
- Appendix II ("Content of the Batch Certificate for Medicinal Products") requires: letterhead of the batch-certifying/releasing manufacturer; name, strength/potency, dosage form and package size; batch number of the finished product; name of the destination country/countries (at least when within the EU); a certification statement ("I hereby certify that all the manufacturing stages of this batch of finished product have been carried out in full compliance with the GMP requirements of the EU and [when within the EU] with the requirements of the Marketing Authorisation(s) of the destination country/countries"); name, signature and date of the Qualified Person certifying the batch.
- This Batch Certificate is a **GMP-compliance attestation by a Qualified Person**, not a laboratory test report of analytical results. Full-text search of Annex 16 for "certificate of analysis" and "CoA" found no occurrences — Annex 16 does not use either term. Where Annex 16 uses "certificate," it means either this QP Batch Certificate or, in Section 1.9 on repackaged/relabelled batches, an analogous QP confirmation of compliance — not a laboratory-issued CoA.
- Annex 16 does describe QP reliance on prior manufacturing/testing records and on the compliance of upstream sites (Sections 1.4–1.7), but it does not impose or reference CoA content requirements; it operates one layer above the laboratory report, at batch release.

### 3. WHO — TRS 1010, Annex 4, "Model certificate of analysis" (2018) — currently in force

Source: WHO Expert Committee on Specifications for Pharmaceutical Preparations, Fifty-second report, *WHO Technical Report Series, No. 1010* (2018), Annex 4, "Model certificate of analysis," and its Appendix 1, "Model certificate of analysis for starting materials and finished pharmaceutical products." Retrieved directly from WHO's CDN: https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/medicines/norms-and-standards/guidelines/quality-control/trs1010_annex4_who_model_certificate_analysis.pdf (primary text read in full). The document itself states this supersedes the model first published in TRS 902 (2002), Annex 10, which is cited only as the historical first version.

TRS 1010 Annex 4 is explicitly framed as **non-prescriptive guidance** ("The format and organization of the information on the CoA is at the issuing laboratory's discretion"; the model form is captioned "This model is intended to serve as an example and not to be prescriptive"). It is intended primarily for quality control laboratories (QCLs) not affiliated with the manufacturer, and draws on WHO good practices for pharmaceutical QC laboratories, WHO GMP for pharmaceutical products, ISO/IEC 17025, and IPEC's excipient CoA guide.

The named data fields it recommends a CoA include (quoted/closely paraphrased from the annex text and its Appendix 1 model form):
- Name and address of the laboratory issuing the CoA
- Identification number of the CoA, with page numbering ("page X of Y") on every page
- Name, address and contact person of the originator of the request for analysis
- Registration/sample number assigned by the laboratory on receipt
- Date received and quantity received
- Name of the product (INN, brand name, etc.), dosage form, strength, package size (for finished products); grade (for starting materials); type and material of primary packaging
- Batch number, date of manufacture (if available), expiry/retest date
- Name and address of the original manufacturer; name and address of the repacker/trader if applicable
- Specifications for testing and reference to the test procedure(s), including acceptance limits
- Results of all tests performed, in numerical form where applicable, compared against acceptance criteria; results of tests performed by subcontractors identified as such
- Any comments/observations/test conditions relevant to interpreting the results
- A conclusion on whether the sample complies with the specification
- Date and signature of the head of laboratory or other authorized person approving the certificate

Additional provisions in the annex text: sampling plan/procedure should be referenced where relevant to validity; a statement of measurement uncertainty should be included where relevant or where a customer requires it (the annex notes neither pharmacopoeias nor medicines regulatory authorities currently require expanded uncertainty to be reported for compliance testing); a customer under contract testing may request additional information be specified; a photograph of the packaging/product may be included if appropriate; ISO/IEC 17025-accredited QCLs should reference their accreditation in the CoA if relevant to the specific analysis; and a statement of expected shipping/storage/distribution conditions may be included when the certificate is used in trade, "deviation from which would invalidate the certificate."

**On reissued certificates (WHO):** "If new certificates are issued by or on behalf of repackers or traders, these certificates should show the name and address of the laboratory that performed the tests and the name and address of the original manufacturer. A copy of the CoA generated by the original manufacturer should be attached." This addresses a repacker/trader issuing its *own* new certificate referencing an upstream one — it does not address correcting an erroneous certificate (see distinct finding below).

### 5. 21 CFR Part 11 — §11.10(b)

Source: 21 CFR §11.10, eCFR (current, as displayed today), Title 21, Chapter I, Subchapter A, Part 11, Subpart B, retrieved via the eCFR content API: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-11/subpart-B/section-11.10 (primary text read in full via eCFR's rendered current text).

§11.10(b), verbatim: "The ability to generate accurate and complete copies of records in both human readable and electronic form suitable for inspection, review, and copying by the agency. Persons should contact the agency if there are any questions regarding the ability of the agency to perform such review and copying of the electronic records."

This provision governs a firm's internal electronic-recordkeeping systems and their reproducibility **for FDA inspection**. Its text is agency-facing ("suitable for inspection, review, and copying by the agency") and does not on its face address, or use language about, a report or certificate issued **outside the firm** to a customer. Part 11 as a whole was searched for "certificate of analysis" and "CoA" in the text retrieved; neither term appears in §11.10. No other clause of Part 11 addressing externally-issued reports was identified in this pass.

## Distinct finding: what happens when an issued certificate turns out to be wrong

None of the primary sources read in this pass — ICH Q7 §11.4, EU GMP Chapter 6, EU GMP Annex 16, WHO TRS 1010 Annex 4, or 21 CFR Part 11 §11.10(b) — contains a procedure, required reason, revision-numbering scheme, or defined status (e.g., "superseded," "recalled," "void") for a Certificate of Analysis discovered after issuance to be wrong.

- ICH Q7 §11.4 addresses only *new* certificates issued by downstream parties (repackers, reprocessors, agents, brokers) that reference an original batch certificate (11.44) — a forward-chain-of-custody provision, not a correction/amendment provision.
- WHO TRS 1010 Annex 4 has the closest analogous clause — "If new certificates are issued by or on behalf of repackers or traders..." — but this, too, describes a repacker/trader issuing a *derivative* certificate that must reference and attach the original, not a laboratory correcting its own erroneous certificate.
- Neither EU GMP Chapter 6 nor Annex 16 discusses amendment or correction of a certificate; Annex 16's Section 1 does discuss what a QP must do when a defect or deviation is identified before certification, but this is about batch release decisions, not about correcting a previously issued CoA.
- 21 CFR Part 11 §11.10 addresses audit trails for changes to electronic records generally ("Record changes shall not obscure previously recorded information," §11.10(e)) but this governs internal record systems, not the status of an externally issued document.

This is reported as a clean negative: across the primary sources examined, no authority defines what "amend," "revise," "supersede," or "recall" means for an issued Certificate of Analysis, nor requires a revision number or stated reason for reissuing one. Any such practice appears to be left to a firm's (or, per WHO TRS 1010 Annex 4, a QCL's) own procedures.

## What I could not establish

- Whether EudraLex Volume 4 Part I Chapter 6 (dated 28 March 2014) has been superseded by a newer official version. The Commission's own EudraLex Volume 4 index page, fetched today, did not state explicitly that the 2014 PDF is the current version, and I found no newer Chapter 6 PDF in this pass. This should be treated as unconfirmed currency, not as a confirmed "still current" finding.
- Whether USP General Notices, or any other USP compendial general chapter, imposes CoA content requirements — the source is paywalled and was not accessed. This scouting pass cannot report on USP's position either way.
- Whether any ICH Q7 Q&A document (the FDA guidance referenced in a 2018 Federal Register notice, "Q7 Good Manufacturing Practice Guidance for Active Pharmaceutical Ingredients: Questions and Answers") adds interpretive detail to §11.4. This Q&A document was identified in search results but its text was not retrieved or read in this pass, so no claim is made about its content.
- Whether any national (non-EU, non-US, non-WHO) regulator imposes CoA content requirements — out of scope for this pass and not investigated.
- Whether ISO/IEC 17025 itself (referenced by WHO TRS 1010 Annex 4 as an input) independently imposes CoA/test-report content requirements — ISO/IEC 17025 is a paywalled standard (iso.org) and was not retrieved in this pass; WHO's annex describes 17025's requirements as having been "taken into account" but does not reproduce 17025's own text, and neither did this scouting pass.
