# Does any open-source LIMS, or FDA's OOS guidance, model a single instrument re-injection that serves both routine QC/SST-trend purposes and an active investigation/diagnostic purpose at once?

**Asked by:** Map-scout dispatch re: `qcDeterminations` (issue #23) / `phaseIObservations` (issue #63) boundary case — an SST bracketing-standard reinjection performed specifically to test instrument malfunction during a Phase I OOS investigation.
**Date:** 2026-09-11
**Status of sources:**
- FDA OOS guidance: confirmed as **May 2022, "Investigating Out-of-Specification (OOS) Test Results for Pharmaceutical Production, Level 2 revision"** — same document already established current in this repo at `docs/research/fda-oos-investigation-structure.md:6` (landing page shows "Content current as of: 08/01/2024," docket FDA-1998-D-0019). I re-pulled the PDF directly (`https://www.fda.gov/media/158416/download`) and ran full-text search (`pdftotext`) against it myself for this question's specific vocabulary, rather than relying only on the prior file's quotes.
- SENAITE: cloned `github.com/senaite/senaite.core` at HEAD (shallow clone, 2026-09-11) and searched source directly — not a wiki, blog, or the marketing site.
- OpenELIS Global 2: cloned `github.com/I-TECH-UW/OpenELIS-Global-2` at HEAD (shallow clone, 2026-09-11) and searched source directly, including Hibernate/JPA mapping files and Liquibase migrations, which are as close to primary as this project's schema gets.

## Findings

### 1. FDA OOS guidance: the two passages exist, but the guidance never connects them

The May 2022 guidance contains both halves of the question, in two separate places, with no cross-reference between them:

- **Routine system suitability** (p.3, under "Responsibility of the Analyst"): "Certain analytical methods have system suitability requirements, and systems not meeting these requirements should not be used. For example, in chromatographic systems, reference standard solutions may be injected at intervals throughout chromatographic runs to measure drift, noise, and repeatability. If reference standard responses indicate that the system is not functioning properly, all of the data collected during the suspect time period should be properly identified and should not be used." This is the paragraph that describes exactly what `qcDeterminations` targets — bracketing-standard injections judged against a criterion.
- **Diagnostic reinjection during Phase I** (pp.4–5, under "Responsibilities of the Laboratory Supervisor," already quoted in this repo at `docs/research/laboratory-equipment-identity-and-calibration-state.md:106`): "Solutions can be re-injected as part of an investigation where a transient equipment malfunction is suspected. Such hypotheses are difficult to prove. However, reinjections can provide strong evidence that the problem should be attributed to the instrument, rather than the sample or its preparation."

I searched the full extracted text of the PDF for "bracketing," "double-count," "double count," and any sentence linking the two paragraphs above (e.g., a statement that a diagnostic reinjection "also serves as" or "counts as" a system suitability check, or vice versa). None of these appear. The two passages sit in different sections, addressed to different points in the workflow (ongoing-run monitoring vs. after-the-fact Phase I diagnosis), and the guidance gives no indication that it has considered the case where a single injection event is claimed as both at once. This is a genuine silence in the text, not a resolved point I am summarizing loosely — I confirmed it by direct string search of the extracted PDF text, not just by reading the prose.

**Conclusion on FDA text: the guidance does not address the dual-purpose case at all.** It supplies the two purposes as separate concerns without saying whether they can or must be reconciled when the same physical injection serves both.

### 2. SENAITE: no mechanism found linking QC records to an investigation entity, and no investigation/nonconformance content type exists in core

- SENAITE's QC content types are `ReferenceAnalysis` (`src/bika/lims/content/referenceanalysis.py`) for control/blank samples and `DuplicateAnalysis` (`src/bika/lims/content/duplicateanalysis.py`) for duplicate injections. Neither schema contains a field referencing any investigation, nonconformance, or deviation content type. `senaite.core` has no content type named anything like `NonConformance`, `Investigation`, `Deviation`, or `QAEvent` — a repo-wide search for `investigat` in Python source only turns up two unrelated hits, both plain-English strings inside e-mail notification templates in `bikasetup.py`/`senaitesetup.py` about samples being invalidated (`src/bika/lims/content/bikasetup.py:935`, `src/senaite/core/content/senaitesetup.py:1123`), not a workflow state or content type.
- The closest analogous *pattern* in SENAITE is the ordinary (non-QC, non-investigation) retest link: `AbstractAnalysis` carries a `RetestOf` UID-reference field (`src/bika/lims/content/abstractanalysis.py:96-98`) with helper methods `isRetest()`, `getRetestOfUID()`, `getRetest()` (lines 1340-1372). This links a retest `Analysis` back to the original retracted `Analysis` — a same-type link (Analysis→Analysis), used for ordinary retraction/retest workflow, not for QC-to-investigation cross-linking. It demonstrates SENAITE's general appetite for "this record supersedes/derives from that one" links, but it is not the QC/investigation case asked about.
- The only place SENAITE gates on QC results at all is instrument-level: `instrument_qc_failures_viewlet.pt` disables an instrument ("Instrument disabled until successful calibration") when QC fails, and disposes it pending "new calibration tests" — a binary instrument-availability gate, not an investigation record, and not a link from a QC result to any diagnostic narrative.

**Conclusion on SENAITE: not found.** No FK, tag, or flag anywhere in `senaite.core` connects a QC/SST record to an investigation-type entity, because no investigation-type entity exists in this codebase at all.

### 3. OpenELIS Global 2: the two record types exist as genuinely separate modules with no live link between them — but one dead/unwired field suggests someone anticipated the problem

OpenELIS Global 2 has both halves of the pattern this question is looking for, as two architecturally distinct subsystems:

- **The nonconformance side**: `qaevent`, `analysisqaevent`, `sampleqaevent`, `analysisqaeventaction`, `sampleqaeventaction` packages (`src/main/java/org/openelisglobal/...`), backed by Hibernate mappings `AnalysisQaEvent.hbm.xml`, `SampleQaEvent.hbm.xml`, `QaEvent.hbm.xml`. `AnalysisQaEvent` is a many-to-one join entity: `ANALYSIS_ID` → `QA_EVENT_ID`, i.e., an ordinary patient/sample `Analysis` linked to a `QaEvent` (nonconformance) record, with a `completedDate`. This module is about pre-analytic/sample-handling nonconformities (see the frontend's `CaseNonconformancePanel.jsx`, `ReportNonConformingEvent.jsx`), not instrument QC.
- **The instrument-QC side**: a separate `qc` package (`org.openelisglobal.qc.*`) with `QCResult` (one row per instrument QC run — `control_lot_id`, `test_id`, `instrument_id`, `result_value`, `z_score`, `run_date_time`, `result_status`), `QCStatistics` (mean/SD over a control lot, computed by `QCStatisticsServiceImpl`), and `QCRuleViolation` (Westgard-style rule violations keyed by `triggering_result_id`).
- **These two modules do not reference each other anywhere in the codebase.** I grepped the `qc` package for any import or reference to `QaEvent`/`qaEvent` and found none. `AnalysisQaEvent` and `SampleQaEvent` likewise contain no reference to `QCResult` or `control_lot_id`.
- **One field looks like it was meant to bridge this gap, and doesn't**: `QCResult.java` declares `private Boolean nonConformityFlag = false;` (`src/main/java/org/openelisglobal/qc/valueholder/QCResult.java:61-62`) with a getter/setter. I grepped the entire `qc` package (services, DAOs, statistics code) for any read or write of `nonConformityFlag`/`non_conformity_flag` beyond the field declaration itself and found **none** — it is set at construction to `false` and never touched anywhere else in `org.openelisglobal.qc`. It is not read by `QCStatisticsServiceImpl.calculateInitialRunsStatistics` or `calculateRollingStatistics`, and it is not populated from, or linked to, the separate `qaevent` module. It is a schema-level placeholder for "this QC result is somehow non-conforming," unwired to anything.
- **The statistics/trend query itself has no exclusion mechanism of any kind.** `QCResultDAOImpl.findByControlLot(controlLotId)` (`src/main/java/org/openelisglobal/qc/dao/QCResultDAOImpl.java:32-43`) is an unconditional `WHERE controlLotId = ?` query with no status filter, and `QCStatisticsServiceImpl.calculateInitialRunsStatistics`/`calculateRollingStatistics` (`src/main/java/org/openelisglobal/qc/service/QCStatisticsServiceImpl.java:44-76`) consume its full result list directly. There is no code path in this module that would exclude an investigation-triggered reinjection from trend statistics even if one were flagged — the `nonConformityFlag` being dead code means, functionally, nothing is excluded from anything.

**Conclusion on OpenELIS Global 2: the two record types (QC result, nonconformance event) exist as separate, unlinked object graphs. A field that gestures at cross-linking (`nonConformityFlag`) exists but is unwired — it is not an FK to an investigation entity, is never set, and has no bearing on the statistics computation.** This is not "solved," but it is closer than SENAITE: OpenELIS at least has both entity types built, and a vestigial hook suggesting the gap was noticed by someone, without being closed.

### 4. Prior art outside LIMS for "one event, two linked record types, no double-counting"

I did not find, in either codebase, any explicit rule (comment, docstring, test, or SOP-shaped text) stating that an investigation-triggered QC rerun must be excluded from trend/control-chart/replicate-count statistics. Neither codebase's QC statistics layer contains any conditional logic keyed to investigation status at all — SENAITE because it has no investigation entity to key on, OpenELIS because the two modules don't communicate.

I did not locate primary documentation, in the course of this specific search, from a system outside LIMS/ELN scope (e.g., manufacturing MES, clinical-trial EDC) describing this same "one physical event, two typed records, must-not-double-count" pattern with citable source text. This question's scope note says any regulated-industry system is admissible, but I was not able to find one with primary-source-level documentation in the time available; I did not find a citable analog and am reporting that as a negative result rather than reasoning by analogy to something I have not verified.
