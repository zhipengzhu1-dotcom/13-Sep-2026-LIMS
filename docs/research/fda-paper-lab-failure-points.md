## What 21 CFR 211.194 requires a laboratory record to contain

21 CFR 211.194(a) ("Laboratory records") requires laboratory records to include complete data from all tests, specifically:

1. **(a)(1)** A description of the sample received for testing: source/location, quantity, lot number or distinctive code, date sample was taken, and date sample was received for testing.
2. **(a)(2)** A statement of each method used, with the location of data establishing the method's accuracy/reliability, and confirmation that method suitability was verified under actual conditions of use.
3. **(a)(3)** A statement of the weight or measure of sample used for each test, where appropriate.
4. **(a)(4)** A complete record of all data secured during each test, including all graphs, charts, and spectra from laboratory instrumentation, identified to the specific component/container/closure/in-process material/product and lot.
5. **(a)(5)** A record of all calculations performed in connection with the test, including units of measure, conversion factors, and equivalency factors.
6. **(a)(6)** A statement of test results and how they compare to established standards of identity, strength, quality, and purity.
7. **(a)(7)** The initials or signature of the person who performs each test and the date(s) performed.
8. **(a)(8)** The initials or signature of a second person showing the original records have been reviewed for accuracy, completeness, and compliance with established standards.

Beyond (a), 211.194 also requires: (b) complete records of method modifications and the data justifying them; (c) complete records of testing/standardization of reference standards, reagents, and standard solutions; (d) complete records of periodic instrument calibration per §211.160(b)(4); (e) complete records of all stability testing under §211.166.

Related sections:
- **§211.180(c)**: all required records (or copies) must be readily available for inspection at the site where the activity occurred, and subject to photocopying/reproduction; records retrievable electronically from another location satisfy this.
- **§211.180(d)**: records may be retained as originals or as **true copies** (photocopies, microfilm, microfiche, or other accurate reproductions); where reduction techniques are used, suitable reader/photocopy equipment must be available.
- **§211.180(e)**: records must support at least annual evaluation of drug product quality standards, including review of a representative sample of batches and of complaints/recalls/returns/investigations.
- **§211.68(a)-(c)**: automatic/mechanical/electronic equipment (including computers) may be used if routinely calibrated/checked per written program, with records maintained; input/output must be checked for accuracy at a frequency based on system complexity/reliability; a backup file of data entered must be maintained (except where certain calculation data are eliminated by computerization, in which case a written record of the program plus validation data must be kept); and **§211.68(c)** allows automated equipment to satisfy "one performs, one checks" requirements (e.g., §§211.101(c)/(d), 211.103, 211.182, 211.188(b)(11)) only if the equipment is validated for that purpose and a person checks that it performed the operation correctly.

**Read plainly, this is a large amount of hand-transcribed content per sample-test:** sample identity/provenance, method reference, sample weight, every raw datum (including graphs/spectra), every calculation with conversion factors, the result and its comparison to spec, and two sets of initials/dates — all of which, in a paper worksheet, must be written by hand or copied from an instrument printout onto that worksheet.

## How FDA/PIC/S characterise hybrid systems, spreadsheets and transcription

**FDA, Data Integrity and Compliance With Drug CGMP (Dec. 2018), Q&A format — https://www.fda.gov/media/119267/download:**

- On discarding working paper after transcription: *"It is not acceptable to record data on pieces of paper that will be discarded after the data are transcribed to a permanent laboratory notebook (see §§ 211.100(b), 211.160(a), and 211.180(d))."* (Q&A on data recording practices.) This is a direct FDA statement, not an inference — the guidance names the exact failure mode (scratch paper → transcription → discard) as noncompliant.
- On paper printouts as "original record" vs "true copy" (Q10): *"A paper printout or static record may satisfy retention requirements if it is the original record or a true copy of the original record... During data acquisition, for example, pH meters and balances may create a paper printout or static record as the original record. In this case, the paper printout or static record, or a true copy, must be retained."* But: *"electronic records from certain types of laboratory instruments—whether stand-alone or networked—are dynamic, and a printout or a static record does not preserve the dynamic record format that is part of the complete original record... a static record or printout is fixed and would not satisfy CGMP requirements to retain original records or true copies... if the full spectrum is not displayed in the printout, contaminants may be excluded."* FDA also states that original laboratory records, paper or electronic, must be subject to second-person review under §211.194(a)(8).
- On static vs. dynamic: *"static is used to indicate a fixed-data record such as a paper record or an electronic image, and dynamic means that the record format allows interaction between the user and the record content."* (E.g., a chromatogram is dynamic and reprocessable; a printout of it is static and may hide information.)
- On audit trail review: audit trail review is likened to *"assessing cross-outs on paper when reviewing data"* and should be done by the person responsible for record review as part of that review.
- On shared logins: FDA states that when credentials are shared, a unique individual performing an action cannot be identified.
- The guidance does **not** contain the word "hybrid" and does **not** discuss spreadsheets by name at all — this is a gap relative to the question, filled by PIC/S below.

**PIC/S PI 041-1, Good Practices for Data Management and Integrity in Regulated GMP/GDP Environments (1 July 2021) — https://picscheme.org/docview/4234:**

- Defines a hybrid system (glossary, p. 60ish): *"A system for the management and control of data that typically consists of an electronic system generating electronic data, supplemented by a defined manual system that typically generate[s] a paper-based record. The complete data set from a hybrid system therefore consists of both electronic and paper data together. Hybrid systems rely on the effective management of both sub-systems for correct operation."*
- Section 9.10, "Management of Hybrid Systems": *"Hybrid systems require specific and additional controls in reflection of their complexity and potential increased vulnerability to manipulation of data. For this reason, the use of hybrid systems is discouraged and such systems should be replaced whenever possible."*
- The same section explicitly calls out the transcription interface as a control point: *"Procedures and records should be available to manage and appropriately control the interface between manual and automated systems, particularly steps associated with: manual input of manually generated data into computerised systems; transcription (including manual) of data generated by automated systems onto paper records; and automated detection and transcription of printed data into computerised systems."* And instructs inspectors: *"Inspectors should verify that adequate controls and secondary checks are in place where manual transcription between systems takes place. Original data should be retained following transcription and processing."*
- PI 041-1 does **not** contain a dedicated section on GMP-calculation spreadsheets by that name; the only textual hit for "spreadsheet" in the document is a passing example of audit-trail export functionality (§9.x, p.46), not a substantive treatment of spreadsheet controls. **This is a gap** — the "spreadsheets used for GMP calculation" theme in this research is better evidenced by warning letters (below, evidentiary weight: FDA observation) than by PI 041-1's own text.

**FDA, Part 11, Electronic Records; Electronic Signatures — Scope and Application (August 2003) — https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/part-11-electronic-records-electronic-signatures-scope-and-application:**

- Confirms paper/electronic co-existence is contemplated by FDA as a "hybrid" situation: *"paper and electronic record and signature components can co-exist (i.e., a hybrid situation) as long as predicate rule requirements are met and the content and meaning of those records are preserved."* Footnote gives examples: paper records plus electronic records, paper records plus electronic signatures, or handwritten signatures executed to electronic records. This is the narrowest, most literal FDA definition of "hybrid" located (2003) — it predates and differs slightly in emphasis from PIC/S's fuller 2021 definition, but the two are consistent.

## Recurring observed failure points in paper/hybrid QC labs

Labeled by evidentiary weight: **[REQUIRES]** = regulatory text, **[OBSERVED]** = FDA's own stated observation/guidance characterization, **[ASSERTED]** = secondary/industry commentary only.

- **[OBSERVED]** Recording data on scrap paper, later transcribed to the "official" notebook and discarded — named directly and prohibited in the 2018 Data Integrity Q&A (see quote above).
- **[OBSERVED]** Static printouts/paper copies of dynamic instrument records (e.g., chromatograms, spectra) that omit information present in the original dynamic file (2018 Q&A, Q10) — a specific, named risk of the "instrument prints, technician transcribes the printed number" step.
- **[OBSERVED]** Shared login credentials preventing attribution of who performed an action (2018 Q&A, Q5).
- **[OBSERVED]** Inadequate or absent audit trail review as part of second-person record review (2018 Q&A, Q7-Q8).
- **[OBSERVED, PIC/S]** Manual transcription at the boundary between manual and computerized sub-systems, both directions (manual data into computer systems; computer-generated data transcribed onto paper) flagged by PIC/S as the specific point requiring "adequate controls and secondary checks" (PI 041-1 §9.10).
- **[ASSERTED, secondary sources pointing to FDA warning letters]** Uncontrolled/unqualified Excel spreadsheets used for GMP calculations, with unrestricted analyst ability to modify or delete files, is described by industry secondary sources (e.g., Ofni Systems, a compliance vendor summarizing an FDA warning letter; a 2025 trade summary citing "14 FDA warning letters" with data-integrity citations) as a recurring citation theme in FDA warning letters. **I was not able to independently pull the primary warning letter text for these specific citations in this session** (FDA's warning letter database was not directly queried for full letter text) — this is reported as a secondary-source claim about primary-source frequency, not a confirmed primary-source count. The general category (spreadsheets used for calculation are subject to CGMP recordkeeping and computer-system control requirements — §211.68) is a **[REQUIRES]** matter; the *frequency* of citation is **[ASSERTED]**, not independently verified here.
- **[ASSERTED]** "Trial" or unofficial test injections/results recorded off the books, then not reported if unfavorable, is a commonly discussed data-integrity theme in industry literature and FDA enforcement summaries; I did not locate and confirm the exact primary warning-letter text for this specific pattern in this session, so it is reported as a recurring theme with **frequency not quantified in primary sources** examined here.

## OOS Phase I timing constraints on the bench workflow

**FDA, Investigating Out-of-Specification (OOS) Test Results for Pharmaceutical Production, May 2022, Revision 1 — https://www.fda.gov/media/158416/download** (confirmed current; language unchanged from the October 2006 original on this point):

- *"The first phase of such an investigation should include an initial assessment of the accuracy of the laboratory's data. Whenever possible, this should be done before test preparations (including the composite or the homogenous source of the aliquot tested) are discarded. This way, hypotheses regarding laboratory error or instrument malfunctions can be tested using the same test preparations."*
- *"Analysts should check the data for compliance with test specifications before discarding test preparations or standard preparations. When unexpected results are obtained and no obvious explanation exists, test preparations should be retained, if stable, and the analyst should inform the supervisor. An assessment of the accuracy of the results should be started immediately."*

This is a **[REQUIRES/RECOMMENDS]** (nonbinding guidance, not a regulation, but it is FDA's stated expectation) sequencing constraint: the analyst must check the result against spec, and if it is OOS or unexpected, retain the physical preparation and notify a supervisor **before** discarding anything — i.e., before the bench is cleared and before the workflow can move on to writing up the result. In a paper workflow this means the "am I done with this sample" decision point on the bench is gated by a spec-check that must occur while the physical preparation still exists, not later after paperwork is filed.
