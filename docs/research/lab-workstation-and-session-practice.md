# What is documented about how computers are physically shared on a pharmaceutical QC laboratory floor, whether logins are shared in practice, and what session/inactivity behaviour regulators actually require?

**Date:** 2026-09-11

## Findings

### 1. Shared logins as a real, quotable Warning Letter finding

Two examples, retrieved directly from their `fda.gov` warning-letter pages on 2026-09-11 (company names withheld per this repository's no-real-organisation-names rule; described by letter date instead):

- **A Warning Letter dated 23 July 2020** states, regarding a laboratory's infrared spectrophotometer (IR) system: *"your Infrared Spectrophotometer (IR) system did not have adequate access controls and was vulnerable to alteration or deletion of data by analysts. Laboratory employees shared a common log-in and password to access the system."* (Source page: `https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/compliance-actions-and-activities/warning-letters (search by letter date)` — retrieved via direct fetch of the FDA page; the firm's own name appears in the URL/page title as public record but is not repeated in this file's body text per the no-real-names rule.)
- **A Warning Letter dated 17 April 2024** states, regarding a laboratory's HPLC data system: *"You failed to exercise appropriate controls over your high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) software system, such as requiring employee-specific logins, appropriate assignment of administrative privileges, and ensuring the audit trails are enabled."* (Source page: `https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/compliance-actions-and-activities/warning-letters (search by letter date)` — retrieved via direct fetch of the FDA page.)

Both are examples of an **inspector's observed finding**, not new regulatory text — the citations they rest on are the same Part 211/Part 11 provisions already quoted in `account-controls-regulatory-text.md` (chiefly the attributability requirements of §211.194(a)(7)/(8) and the access-control language FDA's 2018 Q&A ties to §211.68(b)). Secondary industry write-ups (ECA Academy, GMP-compliance.org, Certivo, and others) describe additional letters using near-identical "shared common log-in and password" language for HPLC and GC systems; those write-ups were used only to identify which letters to go retrieve directly, and are not cited here as the source of the quotes — the quotes above were re-confirmed against the FDA page itself, not taken from the secondary write-up's paraphrase.

### 2. The shared read-only exception — confirmed verbatim

`account-controls-regulatory-text.md` already quotes this in full from FDA's December 2018 Data Integrity Q&A, Question 5: *"Shared, read-only user accounts that do not allow the user to modify data or settings are acceptable for viewing data, but they do not conform with the part 211 and 212 requirements for actions, such as second person review, to be attributable to a specific individual."* Re-checked directly against `https://www.fda.gov/media/119267/download` on 2026-09-11: the wording is confirmed exact. **The map's belief is correct as stated**: this guidance draws the line at write capability — a shared account that cannot modify data or settings is explicitly carved out as acceptable for viewing; a shared account with modify capability is treated as non-conforming because it cannot support the attributable-review requirement.

### 3. Inactivity / automatic logout — no primary pharmaceutical text states a number

Re-confirming and consolidating what `account-controls-regulatory-text.md` already established, with the PIC/S clause quoted there in full:

- **21 CFR Part 11**: no mention of inactivity timeout or automatic logoff anywhere in its text (confirmed there).
- **21 CFR 211.68** and the rest of Part 211: no mention.
- **FDA's December 2018 Data Integrity Q&A**: no mention (confirmed there by full-text search for "session," "timeout," "inactiv," "log off/logoff").
- **EU GMP Annex 11 (Revision 1, in force since 30 June 2011)**: §12 ("Security," quoted in full in the prior report) contains no inactivity-timeout clause.
- **PIC/S PI 041-1, §9.5 (1 July 2021, confirmed final, not the superseded 2018 draft)**: is the *only* one of these five texts that requires an inactivity logout, and it gives **no number**. Quoted there: *"Computerised systems giving access to GMP/GDP critical data or operations should have an inactivity logout... The time should be shorter, rather than longer and should typically be set to prevent unauthorised access to systems."* "Shorter, rather than longer" is qualitative language, not a figure. **The map's belief that PI 041-1 §9.5 is the only text requiring an inactivity logout, and that it gives no number, is confirmed correct** against these five sources.

**No binding pharmaceutical GMP/data-integrity text checked here states a concrete minute value for inactivity logout.**

**Non-pharmaceutical general security standards — for context only, not binding here:**

- **NIST SP 800-53 (Rev. 5), control AC-11 ("Session Lock")** requires a session lock *"after [Assignment: organization-defined time period] of inactivity"* — the control itself is an assignment/placeholder, not a fixed figure; the organization implementing the control chooses the number (per CSF Tools' rendering of the control text, cross-checked against Tenable's audit-reference rendering of the same clause; the full NIST SP 800-53 catalog text was not independently re-fetched from `csrc.nist.gov` in this pass).
- **NIST SP 800-63B-4** — already quoted in full in `node-password-hashing-and-sessions.md` — does give concrete ceilings, but for a different context (federal digital-identity assurance levels, not lab GMP): AAL2 inactivity timeout "SHOULD be no more than 1 hour," AAL3 "SHOULD be no more than 15 minutes."
- **PCI DSS v4.0, Requirement 8.2.8** — per two independent secondary sources (KirkpatrickPrice, PCI DSS Guide) both citing the clause number and describing identical text — requires re-authentication *"if a user session has been idle for more than 15 minutes."* This is a payment-card-data standard, not a pharmaceutical one, and the full v4.0 standard text itself was not independently re-fetched (it sits behind a PCI SSC portal registration), so this figure is reported at one remove from the primary document and should be treated accordingly if it needs to be load-bearing.
- **CIS Critical Security Control 4.3** — per the same class of secondary source, caps general-purpose OS session locks at 15 minutes idle (2 minutes for mobile devices); the CIS Controls v8.1 primary document itself was not independently re-fetched in this pass.

None of these three general-security figures are binding on a pharmaceutical QC laboratory; they are reported only because the task asked, if a number is wanted, where one would have to come from — no pharmaceutical GMP or data-integrity text supplies one.

### 4. Workstation provision in labs — thin primary-source coverage

- **No primary regulatory or PIC/S text located in this pass describes the physical arrangement of laboratory computers** (one PC per analyst vs. shared bench terminals vs. instrument-attached workstations). Neither FDA's 2018 Data Integrity Q&A, 21 CFR Part 11, nor PIC/S PI 041-1 (as read for this and the prior report) addresses bench-level hardware provisioning as such; they address *account* and *access* controls irrespective of how many physical machines exist.
- **Instrument-attached CDS workstations as a documented industry convention (not a regulatory requirement):** trade-press technical literature on chromatography data systems (e.g., LCGC/ChromatographyOnline's "Ideal Chromatography Data System for a Regulated Laboratory" series) describes the historical/still-common pattern of a chromatography data system (CDS) running on a workstation dedicated to, and often physically attached to, a single instrument, contrasted with networked client-server CDS architectures that centralize control of multiple instruments from shared clients. This is **secondary trade literature describing conventional practice, not a primary regulatory text**, and is reported here only as a pointer to the shape of the practice, flagged accordingly — no PIC/S or FDA primary document was found in this pass making the same statement in its own words.
- **Warning Letters describing physical/system arrangement:** the two Warning Letters quoted in Finding 1 both identify the finding at the level of a *specific named instrument system* (an IR spectrophotometer; an HPLC data system) sharing one login — this is consistent with, but does not itself establish as a general rule, the instrument-attached-workstation pattern described in the trade literature above. The letters describe what inspectors observed at those firms, not a general provisioning rule.

### 5. Attributability — exact primary wording (cross-reference)

The precise wording behind a "no shared write-capable accounts" rule is already quoted verbatim in `docs/research/account-controls-regulatory-text.md`:

- **21 CFR 211.194(a)(7)**: "The initials or signature of the person who performs each test and the date(s) the tests were performed."
- **21 CFR 211.194(a)(8)**: "The initials or signature of a second person showing that the original records have been reviewed for accuracy, completeness, and compliance with established standards."
- **21 CFR 11.10(d)**: "Limiting system access to authorized individuals."
- **21 CFR 11.10(g)**: "Use of authority checks to ensure that only authorized individuals can use the system, electronically sign a record, access the operation or computer system input or output device, alter a record, or perform the operation at hand."
- **EU GMP Annex 11 §12** (Revision 1, in force since 30 June 2011): §12.3 "Creation, change, and cancellation of access authorisations should be recorded." §12.4 "Management systems for data and for documents should be designed to record the identity of operators entering, changing, confirming or deleting data including date and time."

This report does not re-verify these quotes independently beyond what the prior report already confirmed against eCFR and the European Commission PDF; they are repeated here only for completeness of this ticket's specific ask, and should be treated as sourced to the prior report's retrieval.

## What this does not answer

- **No count of how many Warning Letters cite shared logins, or what fraction of Warning Letters do.** Secondary sources (blog posts, compliance-consultancy write-ups) assert figures like "60–80% of FDA drug GMP warning letters" cite data-integrity deficiencies generally; none of those figures were independently verified against FDA's own warning-letter database in this pass, and they are not repeated here as fact.
- **No primary source states how many general-purpose workstations a typical QC lab has, or what ratio of analysts to computers is conventional.** The trade literature located describes CDS-instrument architecture, not general-purpose bench PCs used for LIMS, email, or other software; no primary or secondary source found in this pass addresses that question directly.
- **PCI DSS v4.0 §8.2.8 and CIS Control 4.3 were not independently re-fetched from their own primary publications** (PCI SSC document portal; CIS Controls v8.1 document) — the 15-minute figures for both are reported at one remove, from two independently-sourced secondary descriptions that agree with each other, and are flagged as lower-confidence than a direct-quote retrieval, consistent with this repository's practice of flagging non-`curl`-verified quotes.
- **Whether any other, non-PIC/S pharmaceutical inspectorate guidance (e.g., MHRA's own "GXP" data-integrity guidance, or Chinese/Japanese equivalents) states a numeric inactivity-timeout value** was out of scope for this pass — only the five texts named in the task (Part 11, Part 211, FDA's 2018 guidance, Annex 11, PI 041-1) were checked, and PI 041-1 is the only one of those five with any inactivity-logout clause at all.
- **Whether the two Warning Letters quoted above resulted in FDA follow-up findings that the firms' corrective action was adequate**, or any later re-inspection outcome, was not investigated — this report addresses only the cited language at the time of the letter.
