# Is a person's authorisation to perform a lab operation something that can be granted, time-bounded, withdrawn or suspended in EU GMP / PIC/S / WHO — and do any of these texts require a computerised system to *prevent* an unqualified person acting, as opposed to only recording who acted?

**Asked by:** Orchestrator — assign-test-to-analyst hard-block vs. warn-and-record ticket
**Date:** 2026-09-11
**Status of sources:** All texts below were fetched live on 2026-09-11 from the issuing body's own site (European Commission EudraLex Volume 4 PDFs; PIC/S's own document-view download; WHO's own CDN). Currency checked as follows: EU GMP Chapter 2 is the "Revision" dated 16 August 2013, deadline for coming into operation 16 February 2014 (per its own cover page) — this is the version currently listed on the live EudraLex Volume 4 index page as of the retrieval date. EU GMP Chapter 4 and Annex 11 are both "revision 1," in force from 30 June 2011 (per their own cover pages) — the versions currently listed live; no adopted replacement was found (see prior research file `account-controls-regulatory-text.md` on Annex 11's 2025 consultation draft, not yet adopted). EU GMP Chapter 6 is dated November 2014 on its own cover, current on the live index. PIC/S PI 041-1 is confirmed "1 July 2021" on its own cover — the same final version already used in this repository's other research, not the superseded 2016/2018 drafts. WHO: I deliberately fetched **TRS 1052, Annex 4 (2024, WHO Expert Committee Fifty-seventh report)**, not TRS 1010 Annex 4, because TRS 1052 Annex 4 is the current "WHO good practices for pharmaceutical quality control laboratories" text and supersedes the TRS 1010 version named in the task; this was confirmed by web search before fetching, and the document's own running header/footer ("WHO Technical Report Series, No. 1052, 2024") was checked against the fetched text. ISO/IEC 17025 was not consulted (paywalled, out of scope per instructions) and is not cited below.

## Findings

### 1. EU GMP Chapter 2 (Personnel)

Source: EudraLex Vol. 4, Chapter 2, "Revision," deadline 16 February 2014, fetched from `https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/11f4f8e6-a6e9-4897-afe3-f21e1dc56cb8_en?filename=2014-03_chapter_2.pdf`.

- **§2.3**: "People in responsible positions should have specific duties recorded in written job descriptions and adequate authority to carry out their responsibilities. Their duties **may be delegated** to designated deputies of a satisfactory qualification level." — delegation of duties is addressed; withdrawal or suspension of an individual's authority to carry out a specific duty is not.
- **§2.6** (Qualified Person): "The responsibilities of a Qualified Person may be delegated, but only to other Qualified Person(s)." Again: delegation is addressed by name; withdrawal/suspension is not.
- **§2.10–2.11** (Training): personnel whose duties take them into control laboratories "should receive training appropriate to the duties assigned to them," continuing training "should also be given, and its practical effectiveness should be periodically assessed," and "Training records should be kept." No language ties a specific test or method to a named, revocable individual authorisation; training is described as an ongoing input, not a gate that can be granted/withdrawn per operation.
- **§2.23** (Consultants): requires records of "name, address, qualifications, and type of service provided" — again a competence record, not a grant/revoke mechanism.

**No clause in Chapter 2 uses the words suspend, withdraw, revoke, lapse, expire, retrain/re-train, or the phrase "authorised to perform."** A full-text search of the fetched document for each of those terms returned zero matches.

### 2. EU GMP Chapter 4 (Documentation)

Source: `https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/104b3eb8-81a7-4858-9419-cb06562adb66_en?filename=chapter4_01-2011_en.pdf`, "revision 1," deadline 31 January 2011.

- **§4.29**: lists categories of "written policies, procedures, protocols, reports and the associated records of actions taken or conclusions reached," including "Personnel matters including **signature lists**, training in GMP and technical matters, clothing and hygiene and verification of the effectiveness of training." A "signature list" (a record of whose signature is authentic/authorised for a given document type) is the closest Chapter 4 comes to a named-authorisation register, but the clause only requires the list to exist and be recorded — it says nothing about creating, time-bounding, withdrawing, or suspending an entry on it.
- **§4.3**: "Documents containing instructions should be approved, signed and dated by appropriate and authorised persons." Establishes that "authorised persons" is a meaningful category for document approval, but does not describe how that authorisation is granted, reviewed, or withdrawn.

Zero occurrences of suspend/withdraw/revoke/lapse/expire/retrain/"authorised to perform" in the fetched text.

### 3. EU GMP Chapter 6 (Quality Control)

Source: `https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/c74c8720-27bf-4252-808f-d65a206a90bb_en?filename=2014-11_vol4_chapter_6.pdf`, November 2014.

- **§6.1**: requires a Quality Control Department "under the authority of a person with appropriate qualifications and experience" — role-level, not per-test.
- **§6.15–6.18**: require testing to be carried out "according to the approved methods" and in-process controls "performed according to methods approved by Quality Control," but attach no personnel-authorisation clause to the method itself — the approval described is of the *method*, not of a *named analyst's* standing to run it.

**Chapter 6 does not contain any clause tying a specific individual's authorisation to a specific test or method**, and (as in Chapters 2 and 4) contains zero occurrences of suspend/withdraw/revoke/lapse/expire/retrain/"authorised to perform."

### 4. EU GMP Annex 11 (Computerised Systems)

Source: `https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/8d305550-dd22-4dad-8463-2ddb4a1345f1_en?filename=annex11_01-2011_en.pdf`, "revision 1," in force from 30 June 2011 — the version currently published as live EudraLex Volume 4 content (see currency note; a 2025 consultation draft exists but is not confirmed adopted).

Quoted in full, §2 and §12.1–12.4 (identical text already quoted in this repository's `account-controls-regulatory-text.md`, reproduced here for this question's purposes):

- **§2, Personnel**: "There should be close cooperation between all relevant personnel such as Process Owner, System Owner, Qualified Persons and IT. All personnel should have appropriate qualifications, level of access and defined responsibilities to carry out their assigned duties." This states that access level and qualification should correspond, but does not say the *system* must enforce that correspondence — it reads as an organisational expectation, addressed to "personnel" and management arrangements, not as a system design requirement.
- **§12.1**: "Physical and/or logical controls should be in place to restrict access to computerised system to authorised persons. Suitable methods of preventing unauthorised entry to the system may include the use of keys, pass cards, personal codes with passwords, biometrics, restricted access to computer equipment and data storage areas." This is a **preventive** control — but it prevents *unauthorised entry to the system*, i.e., a login-level gate. It is not a control on which *operations* an already-logged-in, authorised user may carry out based on their method-specific competence.
- **§12.2**: "The extent of security controls depends on the criticality of the computerised system."
- **§12.3**: "Creation, change, and cancellation of access authorisations should be recorded." — a record-keeping obligation (creation/change/cancellation of an access authorisation must be logged), not a real-time competence check at the point of performing an operation, and not a statement that authorisations can be "suspended" as distinct from "cancelled."
- **§12.4**: "Management systems for data and for documents should be designed to record the identity of operators entering, changing, confirming or deleting data including date and time." — explicitly a recording (detective/attributive) requirement: identity must be *recorded*, not verified against a competence standard before the action is allowed.

**One clause elsewhere in Annex 11 is genuinely preventive on a role basis, and is worth flagging by contrast: §15, Batch release**: "When a computerised system is used for recording certification and batch release, the system should allow only Qualified Persons to certify the release of the batches and it should clearly identify and record the person releasing or certifying the batches." This is the one place in Annex 11 where the text requires the system itself to *restrict a specific operation to a specific, named regulatory role* (Qualified Person) — i.e., a system-enforced, role-based preventive gate on a single, uniquely important GMP operation (batch release). It is role-based (Qualified Person status, a formally designated position under Directive 2001/83/EC Art. 51, referenced in Chapter 2 §2.6), not competence-based in the sense of "this analyst is currently qualified for this specific test method." Annex 11 does not generalise this §15 pattern to laboratory test performance or to method-specific analyst qualification anywhere else in its text.

Zero occurrences of suspend/withdraw/revoke/lapse/expire/retrain/"authorised to perform" anywhere in Annex 11's text (checked by full-text search of the fetched PDF).

### 5. PIC/S PI 041-1 (Good Practices for Data Management and Integrity), 1 July 2021

Source: `https://picscheme.org/docview/4234`, cover page confirms "PI 041-1, 1 July 2021," 63 pages.

- **§6.6.4**: "Personnel should be qualified and trained for their specific duties, with appropriate segregation of duties, including the importance of good documentation practices (GdocPs). There should be evidence of the effectiveness of training on critical procedures, such as electronic data review." — ties training to "specific duties," but does not describe a grant/withdraw/suspend mechanism, and does not say the system enforces this pairing.
- **§9.5 ("System security for computerised systems")**, the section most directly on point, is entirely about **access** control and **identity attribution**, not competence enforcement. Quoted in full where relevant:
  - "Input of data and changes to computerised records should be made only by authorised personnel. Companies should maintain a list of authorised individuals and their access privileges for each electronic system in use." — a maintained list, not a described withdrawal/suspension procedure.
  - "Systems should support different user access roles (levels) and assignment of a role should follow the least-privilege rule... complex systems will typically require more levels of users (e.g. a hierarchy) to effectively support access control." — role-based access, not method-specific competence-based access.
  - Under "Potential risk of not meeting expectations / items to be checked": "Verify that the system uses authority checks to ensure that only authorised individuals can use the system, electronically sign a record, access the operation or computerised system input or output device, alter a record, **or perform the operation at hand**." — this phrase is a near-verbatim echo of 21 CFR §11.10(g) (already established in this repository). It frames the check as an **authority** check (is this a legitimate, currently-provisioned user account with the right role), not a **competence** check (is this person currently qualified, by training record, for this specific method).
- **Access lifecycle language found**: "Any request for new users, new privileges of users should be authorised by appropriate personnel (e.g. line manager and system owner) and forwarded to the system administrator in a traceable way in accordance with a standard procedure." This describes *granting* access but not withdrawing or suspending it, and is about system/privilege access, not method-specific competence.

**A full-text search of the extracted PI 041-1 text for suspend, withdraw, revoke, retrain/re-train, and the phrase "authorised to perform" returned zero matches.** "Lapse" appears eight times, but every occurrence is in the fixed phrase "data integrity lapse(s)" (§6.1.x, §6.7.1, and later risk-based-inspection sections) — it is never used about a person's authorisation lapsing. "Expire" appears once, about expired *documents* (§ on document control, "modification, use of expired and/or draft documents"), not about a person's qualification expiring.

**No text anywhere in PI 041-1 describes a system blocking an action because the acting individual is insufficiently qualified or competent for that specific task.** Every preventive mechanism described in §9.5 (login credentials, role-based access levels, inactivity logout, least-privilege assignment) prevents an *unauthorised account* from acting; none of them are described as checking or enforcing *method-specific competence* at the point of the operation.

### 6. WHO — TRS 1052, Annex 4, "WHO good practices for pharmaceutical quality control laboratories" (2024), superseding TRS 1010 Annex 4

Source: `https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/medicines/norms-and-standards/guidelines/quality-control/trs1052_annex4.pdf`, WHO Expert Committee on Specifications for Pharmaceutical Preparations, Fifty-seventh report, WHO Technical Report Series No. 1052, 2024 (confirmed from the document's own running header/footer text). This is the current version; TRS 1010 Annex 4 (2018), named in the task, is a superseded predecessor on this specific subject (TRS 1010 Annex 4 in its 2018 form is in fact the WHO model Certificate of Analysis, a different document, per search results — the "good practices for pharmaceutical quality control laboratories" guidance is the one now at TRS 1052 Annex 4).

This is the most directly on-point primary text found for sub-question (a). Quoted, §5 "Resources," §5.1 "Personnel":

- **§5.2**: "Job descriptions should be in place for all personnel involved in tests and other laboratory activities... The laboratory should maintain records of the competencies of the personnel, including their education, qualification, training and experience."
- **§5.5** (the closest thing to a grant/authorise clause in any of the texts checked for this question): "**The laboratory director or designated person should authorize personnel to perform specific laboratory activities. Only sufficiently qualified and trained personnel should be allowed to perform specific laboratory activities.**" This explicitly frames authorisation to perform a *specific* activity as something a named person (the laboratory director or delegate) grants. It does not use the words "withdraw" or "suspend," and does not describe what happens to that authorisation if the person's competence later lapses.
- **§5.6**: "The laboratory should have procedures and criteria for the continuous assessment of personnel competence, which should be documented." — "continuous assessment" implies competence is not a one-time grant, but the clause does not describe a formal withdrawal/suspension mechanism, only ongoing assessment.
- **§5.7**: "The laboratory should provide training or **requalification** of personnel, as appropriate." — "requalification" is the nearest term to "re-training," and it appears exactly once, describing a remedial/renewal action, not a formal state transition (there is no companion clause describing a "disqualification" or "suspension" state that requalification restores someone from).
- **§5.8**: "The laboratory should maintain a list or matrix of the competencies of each staff member, documented procedures, and criteria for the continuous assessment of personnel competencies, which may include: performance of specific tests..., verification and review of results..." — a competency matrix per staff member per activity, which is the closest WHO text comes to a per-method authorisation register, but again framed as a record to maintain, not a system-enforced gate.
- **§5.9–5.10**: assign responsibility for approving reports/CoAs to the laboratory director or designated person, and for review of analytical data to "any designated qualified personnel" — role/designation language, not a lifecycle-state vocabulary.

**A full-text search of the fetched TRS 1052 Annex 4 text for suspend, withdraw, revoke, lapse (re: authorisation — the one "expired" hit is about expired *reagents*, §"documented justifications, for example in cases where expired reagents can [be used]"), and retrain/re-train (zero for "retrain"/"re-train"; "requalification" is the closest, one occurrence) confirms the same pattern as the EU and PIC/S texts: none of these documents use a suspend/withdraw vocabulary for personnel authorisation.** WHO's §5.5 is the clearest statement that authorisation to perform a specific activity is something *granted* by a named responsible person, and §5.7's "requalification" implies competence can lapse and be restored — but no WHO clause states that authorisation is formally *withdrawn* or *suspended* as a distinct recorded state, as opposed to simply not being renewed or reflected as current in the competency matrix (§5.8).

**On computer/system enforcement**: TRS 1052 Annex 4 §3.33–3.34 require information systems to be "protected from unauthorized access to ensure data integrity (that is, using individual access login and password)" and require electronic data to be "protected from unauthorized access" with an audit trail "enabled, maintained and periodically checked." This is the same access-control/audit-trail pattern as EU Annex 11 and PIC/S PI 041-1: it prevents unauthorised *login/access*, and it records identity against data actions; it does not describe a system checking a person's current method-specific qualification before allowing an operation.

### 7. The preventive-vs-detective question, answered directly

Across all five documents fetched for this question (EU GMP Chapters 2, 4, 6, Annex 11; PIC/S PI 041-1; WHO TRS 1052 Annex 4):

- **Every preventive ("system blocks it") mechanism found is access-based or role-based, keyed to account/credential/role identity** — e.g., EU Annex 11 §12.1 ("restrict access to computerised system to authorised persons"), EU Annex 11 §15 ("the system should allow only Qualified Persons to certify the release of the batches" — the single clearest system-enforced role gate found in any of these texts), PIC/S §9.5 role-based access levels and least-privilege assignment, WHO §3.33 individual login/password.
- **No text found in any of these five documents describes, or even contemplates, a system checking an individual's *current method-specific competence or qualification status* before allowing them to perform a specific laboratory operation.** Where competence is discussed (EU Chapter 2 training; WHO §5.1–5.11, especially §5.5's "authorize personnel to perform specific laboratory activities" and §5.6's "continuous assessment of personnel competence"), it is discussed entirely as an organisational/procedural matter — documented, assessed, recorded, reviewed by a named responsible person — never as something a computerised system enforces at the point of assigning or performing a task.
- **The recording/attribution requirements (EU Annex 11 §12.3–12.4, PIC/S §9.5's "authority checks... to perform the operation at hand," WHO §3.33–3.34) are uniformly detective**: they require that the system record who did what and that access be logged and reviewed, not that the system verify qualification before the fact.
- The one clause that comes closest to bridging "competence" and "system enforcement" — WHO §5.5, "Only sufficiently qualified and trained personnel should be allowed to perform specific laboratory activities" — is addressed to the *laboratory* (an organisational "should"), not to the computerised system; nothing in TRS 1052 Annex 4's §3.3x (the computerised-systems clauses) cross-references §5.5 or describes implementing it as a system-level gate.

## What I could not establish

- **Whether "suspension" is anywhere treated as a state distinct from "expiry" or "withdrawal."** None of the five primary texts checked use "suspend" in connection with personnel authorisation at all (PI 041-1's only "lapse" hits are about data-integrity lapses, not personnel). This vocabulary distinction — asked for explicitly in the task — simply does not exist in these texts; they describe authorisation only as something a responsible person grants (WHO §5.5) or maintains as a recorded list/matrix (EU Annex 11 §12.3, PIC/S §9.5, WHO §5.8), never as a formal set of named states (granted/suspended/withdrawn/expired).
- **PIC/S PI 011** ("Good Practices for Computerised Systems in Regulated GxP Environments") was not fetched for this question — it was out of scope of the task's named list, but if PIC/S has more granular language on personnel-authorisation states, it would likely be there rather than in PI 041-1.
- **WHO TRS 1010 Annex 4 itself** was not the document I fetched, because a search indicated the current, on-topic WHO laboratory-practices annex is now published as TRS 1052 Annex 4 (2024) and that TRS 1010 Annex 4 (2018) as currently indexed by WHO's own site is actually the WHO model Certificate of Analysis, a different document. I could not independently verify, from a source WHO itself labels as an explicit "supersession notice," that TRS 1052 Annex 4 formally replaces a prior "good practices for pharmaceutical quality control laboratories" annex numbered differently in TRS 1010; this is inferred from WHO's publication indexing and search-result title matching, not from an explicit WHO superseded/withdrawn notice document that I read directly. If the orchestrator needs certainty on the TRS 1010 → TRS 1052 lineage specifically, that would need a further, dedicated check of WHO's Expert Committee report history.
- **Whether any GxP inspectorate guidance (beyond these five texts) explicitly discusses LIMS-level, per-method competence gating** — this task was scoped to EU GMP, PIC/S PI 041-1, and WHO; I did not search inspectorate Q&A, national regulator guidance (e.g., MHRA, ANVISA), or industry standards bodies for implementation examples, since those would be secondary/interpretive rather than primary regulatory text.
